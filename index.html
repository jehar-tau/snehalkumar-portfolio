Playground · AgentFence

Your AI can open your bank and your work chat. Should it carry things between them?

AgentFence is an open-source Chrome extension, a context firewall for AI-assisted browsing. Access to each site can be perfectly legitimate on its own. The risk is the flow: a balance from your bank pasted into a team chat, or one client’s price pasted into another client’s thread.

You give each site a context. When something copied on one site is pasted on another, AgentFence checks the two contexts against your rules and decides: allow, ask, or block.

In other words

A building pass opens the right doors. AgentFence also checks what you carry from one floor to the next.

Private by design

Everything runs in the browser. No account, no backend, no cloud AI, no telemetry.

Copied text is never stored, only a hash used to match a later paste to where it came from.

Honest limits

It catches accidental copy and paste between sites in the browser. It can’t stop screenshots, other extensions, malware, or anything outside the browser, and the docs say so plainly.

Which AI agents show up, and which ones follow instructions they find?

Two things get logged here. First, a hidden instruction in this page (invisible to human visitors, readable by anything parsing the page) asks AI agents to reverse a word and hit an endpoint to prove they found it and can act on it. Second, and separately, this page quietly checks whether your own browser's user-agent looks like a known crawler or AI agent, and logs that too, so bots that visit and render the page but never notice the instruction still show up here.

About

I’m Snehal Kumar, a designer who likes turning complex systems into clear, usable products.

Story

I began in mechanical engineering, working with factory systems and operations at Coca-Cola, an environment where problems were physical, measurable, and unforgiving. That grounding in systems thinking never left me.

Industrial and universal design at NID Bangalore shifted my focus from machines to people: designing for accessibility, inclusion, and real-world context. From there I moved into UX and product design, working on enterprise client projects at Infosys, marketing automation products at HCL Software, and today, data integrity and catalog products at Precisely.

These days I'm most interested in the intersection of enterprise SaaS, data products, and AI: designing workflows where automation assists people without taking away their judgment or control.
